# Let staff swap their timeslots

You open the schedule fair: staff propose their own swaps, and all you have left to do is arbitrate.

Staff propose their timeslot swaps in self-service. The targeted colleague agrees, you arbitrate, and nothing is applied without your validation.

## Open the fair

The fair opens on a dated period, like the availability collection. Both are opened and closed from Settings, *Edition* tab, and the *Swaps* and *Availability* screens keep a status line.

![The timeslot swaps screen](/images/fr/docs/echanges.webp "The schedule fair as seen from the administration")

## Arbitrate the requests

1. Open *Swaps* (*Share* menu entry). The screen opens on the queue and counts the requests that arrived since your last visit.
2. For each request, accept or refuse.
3. After each acceptance, two follow-ups are offered on the spot: notify the two people, fix the rest of the schedule.

An accepted swap says so in the requester's espace, specifying that it will only enter their schedule once published. Requests that have been sleeping for too long are raised to the organisation (adjustable age alert, night reminder).

If you activate another edition, the swap requests of the edition you leave stay unanswered (the application warns you before switching).

## Measure whether the fair works

The *Statistics* tab answers "is it working?", over its window: the whole edition or two dates.

- Requests created and in progress.
- Three distinct rates: colleagues' agreement, acceptance by the organisation, completion.
- Share of requests validated in advance.
- Response, arbitration, communication and cancellation delays, as median and 90th percentile.
- Breakdowns by day and by stand given up, and the most frequent broken rules.

The aggregates name nobody, and each figure opens the list of requests it counts.

## What the staff member sees

From their espace (see [The staff espace](espace-animateur.md)), the staff member can ask "Who can replace me?" by designating only their timeslot, with nobody in mind. The application looks for the swaps that really hold and sorts them into three families:

- someone frees you;
- you switch on the same timeslot;
- you swap for a timeslot on another day.

The "Swap with" field is searched by typing a name.
