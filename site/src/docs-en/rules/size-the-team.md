# Know how many people to recruit

You know the minimum headcount your stands require, even before entering a staff member, and you know which skills to train first.

Everything is read in *Diagnostic*, *Staffing need* tab, computed from the stands, timeslots and hours alone.

To place this need in the life of a team, see [organising your team of employees and volunteers](/organiser-son-equipe/).

## Read the recruitment sheet

The minimum headcount is **proven**: no smaller team can hold the seats, even when consecutive days are a mandatory rule. The sheet gives:

- the minimum for each game category, ceiling of timeslots per person included, and what would be needed if nobody took on two;
- how many adults are needed at least (the whole headcount on a public holiday, the evenings) and how many minors at most;
- the unavailability the team absorbs each day and each week.

Each day carries its **margin before the solve**: the people available minus the seats, on its tightest band, expandable band by band. It depends on no solve.

Each figure leads to the screen that changes it: a bound leads to the stand hours of the busiest day, or to the row of its rule in Planning rules; each day leads to its stand hours; a bottleneck leads to the skills grid.

## Check the minimum with a real solve

1. In the *Staffing need* tab, start the check.
2. Choose the number of adults and minors of the fictitious team. In the *Advanced settings*, "Confirm by a computation" is ticked: unticked, only the exact bound answers, immediately, with "at least N"; the solve duration is set in the same place.
3. Read the result. The edition's schedule is not touched.

Each trial is recorded in the history with its team, its duration and its result, so you can compare the sizes tried. A history filter isolates them. The solve runs outside the queue: it gives way to any solve that starts.

## Know who to train

The "To train" section is in the Diagnostic's *Risks* tab, with fragility and tension. It lists, game category by game category, those that the need says are short or that fragility shows are held by a single specialist. For each:

- the figures of these two readings and the days under tension;
- a link to the skills grid filtered on it;
- the candidates, ranked by tension days when they are available, then by wish, then by level. They are the beginners and the autonomous staff of the game category, never a referent or an all-rounder.

A game category with no candidate is a recruitment matter. No solve is started, nothing is written, and the section can be exported as CSV.

## Read the schedule by game category

Below the need figures, the "The schedule, typologie by typologie" section reads the saved schedule by game category: seats, hours, vetted staff, vetted but never assigned, assigned without the skill. Every figure leads to the screen where it is acted on. A stand offering several categories counts one seat in each.

## Measure the size of the problem before solving

Before starting, the Need tab shows, in its "Problem volumetry" section, the volumes: staff, seats to fill, timeslots, manual adjustments and, above all, the **hours to fill against the legal ceiling** of what the team can work, unavailability days deducted. A fill rate close to 1 announces an infeasible schedule before a minute of solve time is spent.

## Last year, for information

The Need tab also shows, for each game category, the absence rate and the hours lost the previous year. This measurement comes from [Realised vs planned](suivre-le-jour-j.md#mesurer-le-réalisé-contre-le-prévu).
