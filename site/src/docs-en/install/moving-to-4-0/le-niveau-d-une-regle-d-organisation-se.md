### The level of an organisation rule is chosen per edition

- **Before (3.x)**: every rule had a fixed level, hard, medium or soft, written in the catalogue. The one exception was the days-in-a-row rule, in two forms: `maxJoursConsecutifsTravailles` (weighted, medium level) and `maxJoursConsecutifsTravaillesDur` (hard, off by default). An edition could only weight a medium or soft rule, and switch a rule off or on.
- **After (4.0)**: an organisation rule has a level chosen per edition: **mandatory** (`OBLIGATOIRE`, the hard level, like a legal rule: a plan that breaks it cannot be delivered) or **preference** (medium level, strong, normal or light according to its weight). This covers the medium rules of the organisation quality category, the meal break (`coupureRepasObligatoire`) and the adult beside a minor (`mineurNecessiteEncadrementMajeur`). Legal, assignment, ad hoc and lock rules and the soft preferences have no level to choose. API: `PUT /api/constraints/{name}/niveau` (`GET /api/constraints` adds `niveauReglable` and `niveauChoisi`), MCP tool `modifier_contrainte`, `contraintes.niveaux` section of the YAML scenario (format v4, [details](../comprendre/scenario-yaml.md)). Migration `V149` adds the `obligatoire` column to `ponderation_contrainte` and rewrites nothing: the weight becomes optional so that a row can carry only the level.
- **What you do**: nothing. **Every edition keeps exactly its level and weight, hence its scores**, and old exports read as before. The days-in-a-row rule joins this mechanism, see the next section. A rule made mandatory on an edition that cannot hold it gives an infeasible plan: run the feasibility analysis before computing ([ADR 0111](repo:docs/decisions/0111-le-niveau-d-une-regle-est-le-niveau-de-son-poids.md)).
