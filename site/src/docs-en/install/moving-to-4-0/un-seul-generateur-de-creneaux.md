### One timeslot generator

- **Before (3.x)**: four paths produced the grid in bulk, each with its routes and tools: the **day templates** and their calendar (`/api/journees-types`, nine `*_journee_type` and `*_journees_types` tools), the **recurring series** (`POST /api/creneaux/recurrence`, `creer_creneaux_recurrents`, `previsualiser_creneaux_recurrents`) and the **derivation from the stands' hours** (`POST /api/creneaux/derivation`, `previsualiser_derivation_creneaux`, `generer_creneaux_depuis_stands`).
- **After (4.0)**: one **timeslot generator** ([ADR 0126](repo:docs/decisions/0126-un-generateur-de-creneaux-unique.md)), with three gestures: a **preview** that writes nothing and checks the resulting grid, an **application**, and a **withdrawal** of dates from the calendar. It starts from one of three sources: the stands' hours (their model and their plages), the **templates** (« gabarits ») and a recurring pattern. The day templates become the generator's templates, with their name, shifts and dates: migration `V169` renames their tables (`gabarit`, `gabarit_vacation`, `gabarit_date`) without touching a row. A timeslot typed by hand does not change. The API is `/api/generateur-creneaux` (`GET` for the state, `POST /apercu` and `/application` with a `source`, `POST /retrait`, the templates under `/gabarits`, the calendar under `PUT /calendrier`); five MCP tools replace the twelve: `lister_gabarits`, `modifier_gabarit`, `supprimer_gabarit`, `previsualiser_generateur_creneaux` and `appliquer_generateur_creneaux`. The YAML moves to version 6: the `journeesTypes` section is called `gabarits`, entry for entry. A timeslot the generator creates on a day the edition did not count unfolds the stands' model, like one typed by hand. **The grid produced does not change**: a test replays each generation on every shipped scenario and compares the timeslots with those of 3.x.
- **What you do**: nothing for your data or your files. A template the generator could not apply (no shift, a zero-length shift or the same shift twice) is not converted: the migration stores it, with its dates, in the `journee_type_avant_4_0` table, readable until 5.0, and its dates are no longer governed (their timeslots stay). The screen does not change yet: it goes through the old routes, which remain aliases (`deprecated` in the OpenAPI) until 5.0, with their old keys (`journeesTypes`, `journeeTypeId`), like the twelve old MCP tools. A script or prompt that names them must move to the new names before 5.0 (see [the old tool names](../comprendre/mcp-outils.md#anciens-noms-doutils)).
